Vision brief · November 2026 · draft for discussion

One data platform for SA Government digital services

The mySA GOV app, government websites and the ID service, brought together in one Fabric data lake, so leaders can see how digital services are used, how well they work and what they are worth, today and against three years ago.

One version of the truthEvery measure defined once and used by every report, dashboard and Copilot answer.
Monthly reporting, automatedThe monthly pack refreshes and arrives in email and Teams without manual work.
Live when it mattersA real-time view of sign-ins, failures and outages, with alerts to the right team.
Long memoryRolling windows and comparisons with the same period up to three years ago.

Where we are

This dashboard is a working prototype built on mock data shaped like the mySA GOV app database. It already shows the kinds of measures leadership has asked about: adoption, journeys, service health, location, platform cost and value. The vision below replaces the mock data with real data, adds the government websites and the ID service, and puts the whole Microsoft tenancy to work around it.

Three services, one picture

mySA GOV app

Digital licences and credentials, registration renewals, payments, notifications. Data from the app database (system-versioned tables keep the history of every change) and from Application Insights.

Government websites

Visits, searches, completed online forms and where people drop out. Data from web analytics and the forms platforms behind each site.

ID service

An identity exchange connecting about 20 identity providers, including the Australian Government's myID and the Department for Infrastructure and Transport's Service SA account. People sign in once for basic access and are asked for multi-factor authentication only for sensitive tasks.

What the ID service can tell us, and what it must not

Useful measures: sign-ins by identity provider, success and failure rates, how often step-up MFA is triggered and passed, time to sign in, and provider outages. The Commonwealth system makes a point that identity providers cannot track which services a person uses, so our analytics should work on counts and rates, never on an individual's trail across services. A privacy impact assessment comes before ingestion.

Three ways leaders see the numbers

Monthly

Automated monthly pack

On the first working day of each month the data refreshes, the Power BI report updates, and a PDF and link arrive in email and a Teams channel. An analyst reviews it and adds the commentary; nothing is written for them.

Live

Real-time dashboard

Sign-ins, failed payments and backend status, seconds old. Fabric Activator posts to the on-call Teams channel when a measure crosses its threshold, for example sign-in failures above 5% for ten minutes.

Over time

Rolling windows and comparisons

Rolling 12 months, financial year to date, and the same month one, two or three years ago, all calculated once in the semantic model so every report agrees.

Using the whole Microsoft tenancy

Microsoft has folded much of its data and automation tooling into Fabric over the past two years. Azure Data Factory now runs inside Fabric, Power BI is part of it, and Power Platform reads and writes the same data. Copilot and agents now answer from the same definitions as the reports. With a whole-of-government tenancy already in place, most of the pieces below are available without new procurement, subject to licences and capacity.

Proposed plan

A step-by-step build, so each phase delivers something leaders can use before the next starts.

  1. Now to end of 2026

    Agree the vision

    • Confirm scope, data owners for each source and the ID service partners.
    • Confirm security classification, and check that Fabric and Power Platform are in scope of Microsoft's current IRAP assessment.
    • Start the privacy impact assessment for ID service and location data.
    • Agree the first ten measures and their definitions with business owners.

  2. Phase 1 · first 3 months

    Foundation and the app

    • Fabric workspace and capacity in an Australian region, with Entra groups and Purview labels.
    • Mirror the mySA GOV app database; build Bronze, Silver and Gold layers; load all available history.
    • Semantic model for the headline measures, including rolling 12 months and earlier-year comparisons.
    • Connect this dashboard's Overview and Journeys tabs to the real data; first automated monthly pack.

  3. Phase 2 · months 4 to 6

    ID service and websites

    • Stream ID service sign-in events through Eventstream; real-time dashboard and Activator alerts to Teams.
    • Bring in web analytics for the main government websites.
    • Measures that cross services, such as people who start on a website and finish in the app.

  4. Phase 3 · months 7 to 12

    Value, cost and AI

    • Value and cost measures with finance-owned assumptions, replacing the placeholders on the Value and Cost tabs.
    • A Fabric data agent in Teams, limited to approved measures, for plain-English questions.
    • Power Apps for the events register and target changes, and write-back from reports.

What this means for the dashboard

Development on this prototype should follow the plan rather than run ahead of it:

  • Keep the front end, swap the data. Each card already reads a named view. Phase 1 builds those views in Fabric, behind an API with Entra sign-in, and the mock files retire one tab at a time.
  • Add comparisons where leaders look first. A "compared with" switch (previous period, a year ago, three years ago) on the Overview tab, fed by the semantic model.
  • Add the two new services as tabs once their data lands: ID service (sign-ins by provider, step-up MFA, failures) and Websites (visits, completions, drop-off).
  • Decide the home for each audience. Power BI for analysts and the monthly pack; this dashboard for leadership and presentations; Teams and Copilot for quick questions.

Decisions needed

  1. Scope of phase 1, and which business owner signs off each measure.
  2. Fabric capacity size and who funds it.
  3. Data sharing arrangements with the ID service partners.
  4. Security classification of the combined data, and where the leadership dashboard is hosted.
  5. Who owns the assumptions behind the value estimate: unit costs, attribution and build cost.

Risks to manage

RiskHow we manage it
Combining identity data with service use creates a privacy riskPrivacy impact assessment first; de-identify in Silver; report counts and rates only; follow the Digital ID rules on tracking.
History is not available for three-year comparisonsLoad history in phase 1; use the app's system-versioned tables; agree retention with each source owner.
Fabric capacity costs more than expectedStart small and scale; watch it on the Cost tab; pause non-production capacity overnight.
Numbers disagree between reportsOne semantic model, one definition per measure, an owner for each.
AI answers are wrong or overreachAgents limited to approved measures and permissions, tracked in Agent 365, with answers that show where they came from.
